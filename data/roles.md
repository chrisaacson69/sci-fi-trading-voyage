# Roles — what each hull specialises in

> Chris, 2026-10-17: *"the whole point of this tool is to make less generalizations 'this ship is the
> best ever!' and more into what it specializes in."*

Every ranking in `data/hull_ceiling.csv` collapses a hull to one number against one pirate template,
and a week of comparisons has shown that number change its mind the moment armour, battle row or
target class changes. This page holds the descriptors that actually decide where a hull belongs.
Generated by `lagrange-combat/tools/roles.py` into `data/roles.csv` (144 hulls, each at its own full
ladder); the 26,732-row head-to-head grid is `lagrange-combat/matrix.csv`.

## The descriptors

| field | what it decides |
|---|---|
| **row** | `Tb_cfg_ship.ROW_INDEX` - 0 front, 1 mid, 2 rear. 36 / 77 / 51 hulls. A mid-row hull is screened: battle7 measured the row-1 Reliat A taking **zero** damage while the row-0 Eris HC took 1,543 per ship. Defensive stats on a back-row hull are worth only what the chance of the front collapsing makes them worth. |
| **armour_floor** | Where its biggest ballistic gun stops scaling. Ballistic damage floors at 10% of table damage, so a 93.8-damage torpedo adds nothing past armour 84. **The single most predictive number here.** |
| **keeps** | DPM at armour 240 as a share of DPM at 0. |
| **energy_pct** | Share of damage from Energy mounts, which ignore armour entirely. |
| **reach** | Carries a torpedo (weapon type 6). Chris: torpedoes *"can hit behind the front row esp if there is nothing in the front row that screens the weapon priority"*. |
| **dpm_cp / ehp_cp** | Where its BV comes from. A hull leaning on hit points is worth less in a fight you are winning, because the survivability is never cashed. |
| **repair** | Hit points healed per minute, directly comparable to incoming DPM. |

## The roles Chris named, with the numbers attached

| hull | CP | row | DPM/CP | EHP/CP | floor | keeps | energy | role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quaoar Torpedo** | 6 | 1 | 727 | 7,966 | 378 | 34% | 0% | **reaches past screens** - torpedoes hit the second row when nothing screens the priority |
| **Eris Heavy Cannon** | 9 | **0** | 406 | 8,937 | 324 | 28% | 0% | **front line**, wants healers behind it |
| **Taurus Defense** | 8 | **0** | 301 | 9,362 | - | **100%** | **100%** | **armour-proof** - all energy, immune to the armour cliff |
| **Reliat Stealth** | 4 | 1 | 393 | **23,672** | 216 | 8% | 0% | **fire sponge** - bolt onto a CA fleet to attract fire |
| **Carilion Special** | 5 | **0** | **21** | **13,102** | 32 | 9% | 0% | **evasion tank** - absorbs, contributes almost nothing |
| **Reliat A** | 4 | 1 | 104 | 6,156 | **81** | 8% | 0% | **anti-FF/DD only** |

### Two places the data refines the list

**The Carilion Special is the tank; the Recon is not.** Special 13,102 EHP/CP against Recon's 5,534 -
and the Special does 21 DPM/CP, so it is almost purely a sponge. battle7 bears it out: the Carilion
Recon dealt 6,007 and took 11,132, the only hull in that fleet absorbing more than it contributed.

**The Reliat Stealth's sponge role is narrower than it looks.** Its 23,672 EHP/CP is nine times its
raw hit points, entirely from evasion - but it floors at armour 216 and keeps only **8%** of its
damage at 240. It attracts fire well and contributes little once the enemy has armour, and it sits in
row 1 where the fire may never arrive. Its #1 frigate ranking is an even-fight figure.

## The Reliat A falloff, measured

Chris: *"That reliat A is going to fall off hard at cruisers and higher, but right now, it does lots
of work vs FF and DD."* With his real 51 TP build, against each class at that class's median armour:

| target | median armour | DPM | share of its anti-FF output |
|---|---|---|---|
| FF | 5 | 5,193 | 100% |
| DD | 20 | 4,148 | **80%** |
| CA | 50 | 2,671 | 51% |
| BC | 180 | 487 | **9%** |
| CV | 112 | 487 | 9% |

Not a gentle taper - a cliff at armour 84, past which it contributes 487 DPM and nothing more. No
amount of tech points moves that; `103`/`104` at L5 raised the torpedo from 75 to 93.8 damage and
pushed the floor from 68 to 84, which widens the useful band slightly and does not change the shape.

## What this page cannot tell you

Roles are per-hull; fights are sequential. Which hull screens which, whether a weapon's priority table
lets it reach past the front, and whether the healers out-heal the incoming before the front collapses
are all fleet-level and none of them are modelled. Chris: *"in a perfect world, we would just model
combat as best as possible and monte-carlo fleets."*

Two inputs that would need are now in place and were not a week ago: the hit tables carry a
**`priority`** per target class alongside the hit rate (the mechanism behind the torpedo reach, read
by nothing yet), and `matrix.repair_pm` gives healing in the same units as incoming DPM, so "can the
healers hold the front" is arithmetic.

Craft are not covered here - `roles.py` walks `playable_weapons`, which excludes fighters and
corvettes. Their armour curves are in `data/upgrades.md`.

## Counters: ship-to-ship beats the average (2026-10-18)

Chris: *"if we are going ship to ship, we can model ship to ship instead of using averages... if a
ship has high evasion, is there something out there that counters it?"*

Yes, and the averaged effective-HP figure actively hides it.

### Evasion is per ATTACKER WEAPON TYPE, and one type is always unprotected

A maxed Reliat Stealth buys `EFFECT_AVOID_INC_BY_WEAPON_TYPE` on top of its 70% base:

| attacker weapon type | total evasion | shots that land |
|---|---|---|
| **2 railgun** | **70%** | **30%** |
| 1 artillery / 3 ion / 4 pulse | 85% | 15% |
| 5 missile | 102% | 0% |
| 6 torpedo | 110% | 0% |

It buys +32 against missiles, +40 against torpedoes, +15 against artillery, ion and pulse - and
nothing against railguns or type 8. Per-attacker the retention spread is **29% at best, 13% median,
0% at worst**, so `roles.csv`'s single EHP figure of 8.99x raw hit points is an artefact of averaging
over one pirate team: nearer 3.4x against a railgun, unkillable against pure missile.

**But retention alone is NOT the counter**, and an earlier version of this page wrongly said it was.
Chris: *"a weapon with a low tohit is the best against a ship with insane evasion? ... the best
counter [is] high hitrate and high shots per minute."* Exactly right - what matters is retention
TIMES output. Ranked by damage per command point actually landed:

| attacker | CP | DPM/CP landed | DPM | retention | weapon types |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conamara Chaos Plasma | 20 | **541** | 10,811 | 29% | 5, **8** |
| **Quaoar Railgun** | 6 | **490** | 2,939 | 29% | **2** |
| XenoStinger Tactical | 8 | 322 | 2,579 | 29% | 1, **8** |
| Holy Spirit | 6 | 312 | 1,870 | 21% | 3, **8** |
| Io A | 18 | 280 | 5,033 | **14%** | 1, 3, 5 |
| Alkaid-class Special | 6 | **58** | 350 | **29%** | 2 |

The Alkaid-class is the refutation: joint-best retention at 29%, and **58 DPM/CP** because the gun is
feeble. And the Io A makes the top five on raw output *despite* 14% retention, so brute force
partially substitutes for bypassing evasion.

### Why railgun and type 8 are the gaps: those avoidances are RARE

Avoidance against railguns exists - it is simply scarce. Counting `AVOID_INC_BY_WEAPON_TYPE` prefixes
by the type they protect against: **missile 19, ion 16, torpedo 16, pulse 13, artillery 10, railgun 3,
type 8 three**. So a hull tunes its evasion against the COMMON threats - blank/artillery mounts are
169 of 325 in the game and missiles another 65 - and is left open to the rare ones. Railgun is 18
mounts game-wide and **type 8 is four** (XenoStinger Tactical 350 dmg, XenoStinger AA 25, Holy Spirit
250), which is why those hulls punch so far above their cost here.

The practical counter is therefore a cheap high-rate railgun (**Quaoar Railgun**, 6 CP, 490 DPM/CP) or
one of the four type-8 mounts, not "any railgun". The hulls that cannot touch it at all are pure
missile - Arctos B and C, Alioth-class Type B, Mare Nubium Siege, Guardian Amphibious - which makes
**an all-missile fleet a composition trap** rather than a hull problem.

### The duel grid had to be regenerated, and needed a maxed mode

Chris's other concern: *"my only concern was if the matrix and grid got updated as we added
mechanics."* `matrix.csv` was dated **30 September** and predated every fix of the last week - flat
damage adds, ship-level hit points, module hit points, repair, strategies, the fight-length cap. It is
regenerated.

More important: the grid was **stock-vs-stock**, and weapon-type evasion only exists as an UPGRADE. A
stock Reliat Stealth evades a flat 70% of everything and `ev_by_wtype` is empty, so no counter-play
was visible at all. `tools/matrix.py --max` now fields every hull fully upgraded and writes
`matrix_maxed.csv`. The mechanism then shows up as the thing that does NOT move:

| opponent weapon type | stock grid | maxed grid |
|---|---|---|
| 6 torpedo | 58% | **12%** |
| 1 artillery | 59% | 34% |
| 3 ion | 88% | 65% |
| 5 missile | 73% | 40% |
| **2 railgun** | **59%** | **59%** |

Every type's win rate against the Reliat Stealth falls when it maxes its evasion, except railguns,
which are unchanged because there is no railgun avoidance to buy. Ion's 65% is higher in absolute
terms only because ion-armed hulls are individually strong; the diagnostic is the delta.

**Read `matrix_maxed.csv` for fielded ships and `matrix.csv` for stock**, and regenerate both after any
mechanic change - they are snapshots, not live views, and the 30 September file was wrong for three
weeks without anyone noticing.

### Weapon type 8 is UAV, and almost nothing evades it

Chris, 2026-10-18, confirming the mechanic in game: *"ships have extra evasion against certain weapon
types, and this is backed ingame.. and type 8 are UAV, which are special weapons indeed."*

The data corroborates it. Two of the **four** type-8 mounts in the entire game are literally
UAV-slotted - the XenoStinger Tactical (350 dmg) and XenoStinger AA (25 dmg), both `slot=UAV 刺水母`
with a `uav=` craft id. The other two are hull mounts: the Holy Spirit (250) and the **Conamara Chaos
Plasma (1,200)**.

**Only two prefixes in the whole game grant avoidance against type 8** (`8010` and `8015` x2), against
19 for missile, 16 for ion, 16 for torpedo, 13 for pulse and 10 for artillery. So UAV damage is the
least evadeable damage in the game, and it shows up as the universal soft spot of every evasion build:

| hull | softest against | lands |
|---|---|---|
| Reliat Stealth | **type-8 / railgun** | 30% |
| Carilion Special | **type-8** | 45% |
| Carilion Recon | **type-8** (flat 43% evasion, no type table) | 57% |

This is also why the Conamara Chaos Plasma tops the counter ranking at 541 DPM/CP against a maxed
Reliat Stealth: its main gun is a 1,200-damage type-8 mount, so it is firing the one type the target
could not buy protection against.

**And it explains the meta Chris described** - *"most of the meta is about using aircraft to kill
other aircraft and a high focus on killing carriers"*. Ship evasion barely works against UAV damage,
so carried craft are the reliable way through an evasion build, and killing the carrier is the only
way to stop them. The two halves fit: evasion counters guns, UAVs counter evasion, anti-air and
carrier-killing counter UAVs.

### The two evasion tanks are near-mirrors, and cover each other

| | Reliat Stealth | Carilion Special |
|---|---|---|
| base evasion | 70% | 55% |
| softest | railgun & type-8, 30% | type-8 45%, then missile/torpedo 25% |
| hardest | **missile 0%, torpedo 0%** | **ion 0%**, then artillery/pulse 15% |

Missiles are useless against the Reliat and workable against the Carilion; ion is useless against the
Carilion and fine against the Reliat. **A fleet running both forces an attacker to bring two weapon
types** - which is a composition argument for mixing them rather than massing the better one.

The Carilion **Recon** has no type table at all, just flat 43%, so it is generically slippery rather
than tuned. That matches its EHP/CP of 5,534 against the Special's 13,102.

### Encoding audit

Chris was right to be sceptical that a "slow weapon" like a railgun should be hard to dodge, so the
`TPPP` decode was re-checked: all **36** `EFFECT_AVOID_INC_BY_WEAPON_TYPE` params are four digits with
a lead digit of 1-8 and a remainder that is always a plausible percentage (5, 7, 8, 10, 15, 20, 25,
30), with no outliers. A mis-decode would produce nonsense percentages, so the reading stands. Whether
`WEAPON_TYPE 2` really is "railgun" rests on an earlier session's name mapping rather than a
localisation table, and remains the weaker link.

## The two Quaoars: a worked role split (2026-10-20)

Derived with Chris from the battle reports, the replays and the in-game trees. Full measurement trail
in [battle_reports.md](battle_reports.md). Both are 6 CP destroyers, row 1, and they are **not**
interchangeable.

### Quaoar Torpedo (41102) - anti-cruiser specialist

| | |
|---|---|
| main cluster torp | 437 dmg, 8 shots, 34.4 s cycle, **5 s lock-on**, built-in crit 15%/170% |
| AA launcher | 87 dmg, 4 shots x2, 14.4 s cycle, 8 s lock-on - ignorable on this hull |
| priority 1 | **cruiser, battlecruiser, carrier** (main); aircraft (secondary) |
| hit% vs FF/DD | **55** |

Its anti-CA case is three things, of which the engine prices only the first correctly:

* **`205`** `HIT_RATE_INC_BY_TYPE` +15% vs cruiser/BC/BS/carrier - **gated behind `9206`**, so the
  package is a lumpy ~25 TP commitment rather than an incremental buy.
* **`9206`** the anti-CA strategy: `TRIGGER_TYPE 10 / TRIGGER_PARAM 5`, i.e. **fires whenever the
  target is a cruiser**, `EFFECT_ROUND -1`, `SKILL_PROB 100`. Effects are `DURATION_INC 30` and a row
  carrying **param 60 with no effect id** - invisible. Its real uptime is the share of fire going into
  cruisers, ~100% against a CA fleet and 0% against FF/DD, where the engine uses a flat 1/3.
* **crit**: `161` 30%/+50% enables, `163` adds +40%, 20 TP for +27% **on a mount with no crit**.
  Stacking onto the main's built-in 15%/170% is **unverified** - the four candidate readings span
  EV x1.255 to x2.17, and the engine takes the most generous.

`213` Ammo Stealth (`INTERCEPT_DEC 30`) is dead weight unless the enemy intercepts, and is a GAP in
the engine - so it prices at zero, which is right against non-intercepting enemies and wrong against
carriers.

**Verdict: an anti-CA ship that also handles low-armour capitals.** Against an all-FF/DD enemy
*neither* mount has a tier-1 priority match and the main drops to a 55% hit rate, so it is the wrong
tool for that format.

### Quaoar Railgun (41101) - and why its anti-small kit is a trap

| | |
|---|---|
| rail | 562 dmg, 2 shots, 4,800 ms cycle, **10 s lock-on, and NO lock-on reduction exists on the hull** |
| turret | 75 dmg, 3 shots x2, 10,200 ms cycle, 8 s lock-on |
| priority 1 | cruiser/BC/carrier (rail); **frigate, destroyer** (turret) |
| hit% vs FF/DD | **70**, plus `203`/`204` for +15% more |

On paper that reads anti-small: tier-1 turret priority on FF/DD, 70% base hit, and two +15% hit
upgrades aimed at exactly those classes. **Chris's call is that this is anti-synergy, and the
measurements back him.** A relock costs `max(0, aim - cycle)` - measured at **5.8 s** on this mount
(10,000 lock against a 4,800 cycle) - and relocks are driven by targets dying and moving. Small ships
die fastest, so the format that its upgrades point at is the format that punishes its lock-on hardest:
**7.6 switches x 5.8 s = 27% of a battle lost**, which is the whole of its measured 2x shortfall
against the model.

With no lock-on reduction available anywhere on the hull, that cost is **structural, not tunable**.

**Verdict: wants a stable target set - few, durable targets - which means big ships.** It also never
reaches its damage floor (armour 517, against a fielded maximum of 240), so it holds at high armour
where the torpedo does not. `9101` (`WEAPON_TARGET_NUM0 +1`, multi-target) is its genuine scaling
option. Treat `203`/`204` as covering a weakness, not as defining a role.

### The pair, together

They are anti-symbiotic rather than complementary: both mains prioritise capitals, so fielding both
concentrates fire on the same targets, while the small-ship coverage falls to the rail's turret and
is undercut by the rail's own lock-on. Pick one per fleet against the enemy you expect.
